A short introduction

I grew up in Anpara, an hour down the road from here. My parents live in Chopan now, and the library is in their house. I went to Delhi University for a degree and to Jawaharlal Nehru University for a master's in International Relations, and I manage a research team in Delhi.

Leaving Sonbhadra was possible because of a few things that were not distributed evenly: parents who treated a daughter's education as non-negotiable, enough money for a train ticket, and books. Plenty of people I went to school with were as capable and had none of that.

I don't think of this library as paying a debt. I think of it as sharing a privilege. The difference matters. A debt gets settled and closed; a privilege shared only gets wider. What I had was books and the time to read them. That is the specific thing I can hand over, so that is what this is.

Most of the adult collection is my own — history, politics, education, India, world affairs, and the fiction I grew up on. It stays as a read-here collection rather than a lending one, because those are books I want intact when I move back. Eventually all of them will be on these shelves. They are more useful in a room people can walk into than in a flat in Delhi.

What I want this to become

Right now it is one hall, a few hundred books, Sunday activities and a handful of volunteers. What it has to become is something that does not depend on my being in town — named librarians, fixed days, keys held by people who live here, and undergraduates from the neighbourhood running it because it is theirs.

And past that, a research point. Sonbhadra produces coal, power and cement for the rest of the country, and its own residents are usually the last to be told anything about the water they drink or the air they breathe. People who live here should be able to read up on that, find out who is accountable, and ask that person the question themselves. Being an informed citizen is not a specialist occupation. It just needs somewhere to start.

If you want to help with any of it, or have books, or simply want to know how it is going — write to ApnaPustakalayChopan@gmail.com.

Apna Pustakalay is a personal project. It is not connected to, funded by, or run on behalf of any employer, organisation or political party.
