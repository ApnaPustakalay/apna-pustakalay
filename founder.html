About

अपना पुस्तकालय

What the library is, what you won't find here, and why it's needed in Chopan, Sonbhadra.

What this is

Apna Pustakalay is a small, free library in Chopan, set up in a hall in a family home. It brings together books for children as well as a collection of books for older readers, with a simple space to sit, browse and read.

It is a personal initiative, not an NGO or institutional programme. The idea is to create a library that is open to everyone, where people can discover books, spend time reading, and eventually contribute to and care for the space themselves.

For now, it is one room in one home. The hope is that, over time, it becomes a space the wider community feels is its own.

What you won't find here

No textbooks, no exam guides, no question banks, and no one checking whether you've read enough to pass. Most of us grew up with books that came with a test attached, and somewhere along the way reading started to feel like work. This library is about making space for the curiosity all that testing pushed aside.

Come in with a question, a little curiosity, or nothing at all. Pick up a book because something caught your eye, read at your own pace, and take your time. There's no syllabus, and no pressure to finish what you start.

Where this is, and why it is needed

Chopan is a small town in Sonbhadra, the southernmost district of Uttar Pradesh, where the state meets Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand and Bihar. The district covers 6,905 square kilometres and held a population of about 1.86 million at the 2011 Census. Industry is its main source of income — coal, power, cement — and very little of what it generates stays here.

More than a fifth of the district is Scheduled Caste, and slightly more than a fifth is Scheduled Tribe. Literacy across Sonbhadra stands at 64.03%. Split by gender, the gap is the whole problem: 74.92% of men are literate, and 52.14% of women.

There are schools here. What there is not is anywhere to read once school is over — no library, no reading room, nothing on a shelf that isn't a textbook. A child who wants a story has nowhere to go and find one.

Figures: Census of India 2011, as published by the District Administration, Sonbhadra. Some sources quote a lower district literacy rate because they measure against the total population, including children under seven.
