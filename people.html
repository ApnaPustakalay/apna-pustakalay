The Story

अपना पुस्तकालय

The story behind the idea, and what the library is meant to become.

Sharing a Privilege

I grew up in Anpara, about an hour from here. My parents live in Chopan now, and the library is in their house.

Leaving Sonbhadra was possible because my parents had the means to support my education, treated a daughter's education as non-negotiable, and gave me access to books and time to read them. Plenty of people I grew up with were just as capable and did not have the same opportunities.

I don't think of this library as paying that back. I think of it as sharing a privilege. What I had was books and the time to read them. That is what I can share here.

Most of the adult collection is my own: history, India, education, world affairs, politics and fiction. They are being brought together here for anyone who walks through the door.

— Ragini Kashyap, founder

Where the idea came from

The idea came from a book.

In A Room of One's Own, Virginia Woolf writes about the material conditions that make intellectual freedom possible: money, time, and a room of one's own. She also describes being stopped at the door of a college library because she is a woman.

It made me think about Chopan, and about what it might mean to have a room full of books that belongs to everyone. A place nearby where anyone can walk in, browse the shelves, pick up a book they did not know they were looking for, and read.

Not everything has to be for school. A library can also be a place to read for pleasure, follow a curiosity, or simply come across something new.

What this is meant to become

Apna Pustakalay starts as a free community library in Chopan. Over time, I want it to grow into a larger collection of books, documents and other resources that people can use to understand the world around them and make sense of the place they live in.

For children, students and anyone curious about Sonbhadra, that could mean learning about its history, people, forests, rivers, culture, economy and development. It could also mean discovering interests that lead somewhere entirely unexpected.

The idea is not just to put books on shelves, but to make knowledge available for people to explore, question and use to create something of their own.
